## About

{.lead} A digital museum of **QZone decorations** (装扮), collecting 5,863 items from between 2005 and 2009: skins, pendants, floaties, cursors, title bars and music players.

QZone (QQ空间) is the personal-homepage platform Tencent launched in 2005. At its peak it had over 600 million monthly active users — the largest social network in China at the time. For a generation it was the first page of their own, and what Y2K maximalism and 非主流 ("non-mainstream") looked like on the Chinese internet.

Adobe ended Flash at the close of 2020 and browsers removed it; QQShow was taken down in 2021; the flower-vine garden (花藤) stopped operating in 2022; the decoration mall itself is long gone.

## On internet memory

Everything in this collection comes from the [Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/) and from Tencent's still-online CDNs.

- The project began half in doubt: starting from the Internet Archive Wayback Machine's CDX index, I mapped every decoration-asset URL that had ever been archived — and found that most of them still answer at their original addresses, fifteen years on.
- So I fetched assets straight from their original URLs first, recovering the few dead ones from the Wayback Machine.
- The item ids, Chinese names, release dates, prices and color tags were all recovered from the QZone decoration mall's catalog API.
- The animated cursor files were decoded frame by frame into APNGs that modern browsers can play; the players and Flash pendants run in the [Ruffle emulator](https://ruffle.rs/); the floaties' drifting paths are recovered from Tencent's 2006 front-end code.

The Chinese web moved into closed platforms early and wholesale: login walls, apps, super-apps, mini-programs. What requires an account, the Wayback Machine cannot crawl. The parts of QZone that existed as *files* — the GIFs, the skins, the cursors — survived by accident; the data that lived behind login endpoints — people's layouts, flower gardens, guestbooks — shut down along with the servers.

## Acknowledgements

This project is deeply influenced by my friend Kay Zeng's [qqshow2000.com](https://qqshow2000.com/). She rebuilt QQShow from assets salvaged out of the Wayback Machine; her site also lives inside this museum's QZone dress-up page. Like QQShow, QZone shaped how hundreds of millions of people first imagined the internet, yet remains nearly unknown outside the Chinese-speaking web. We hope this history can be seen by the wider world.

Equally formative were Jordan Eldredge's [Webamp](https://webamp.org/) and [Winamp Skin Museum](https://skins.webamp.org/), which brought the Windows desktop player Winamp back to life by rewriting it. And thank you to artist friends doing internet digital-archive work, including Yufeng Zhao and Lewei Huang's [BannerDepot 2000](https://banner-depot-2000.net/) and Amad Ansari's [Palestine Online](https://palestineonline.net/).

- Artwork: the mostly unnamed Tencent designers who drew these 5,863 pixel pieces. If your work is here, I'd love to hear from you!
